Five days of
becoming a fish

Half a million cells of one species’ first five days, sorted not by where they are but by what they are expressing.

3.3 – 120 hours post fertilisation
after Sur et al. 2023  ·  the DanioCell atlas

The companion to this page follows single nuclei through a microscope and can say, of one cell, that it came from another. This page cannot, and does not try. DanioCell is a census, not a film: cells dissociated from wild-type embryos at closely spaced stages, each one read once, for its transcriptome, and then gone.

What that buys is a different kind of map. Not where a cell went, but what it was being — and because the sampling is dense in time, how the whole vocabulary of “what a cell can be” changes over five days. The plates below ask four questions of it: where the states sit, how long each one lasts, which programs are shared between states that have nothing else in common, and what one inferred trajectory looks like from end to end.

One thing to hold on to throughout. Cells that sit near each other in these plates are expressing similar genes. They are not relatives. DanioCell carries no lineage tracing at all, and no plate on this page — including the trajectory in Plate IV — observed any cell turning into any other.
