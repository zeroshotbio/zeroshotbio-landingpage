A fate map of the
zebrafish gastrula

One embryo, from fifty-per-cent epiboly to the tail bud — every nucleus that could be followed, and the cell each one came from.

5.5 – 11.3 hours post fertilisation

Zebrafish has no invariant lineage. Unlike C. elegans, where the same cell divides in the same order in every animal, a fish embryo mixes and rearranges through gastrulation, and where a cell ends up is a matter of tendency rather than of law. What it has instead are fate maps: statements about which region of the early embryo tends to give rise to which part of the animal.

Those maps have historically been drawn by labelling a handful of cells and looking later. This one is drawn differently. In 2026 Wang and colleagues published a reconstruction of the lineage of an entire zebrafish embryo from 3D time-lapse microscopy — nucleus detections across frames, linked frame to frame into explicit parent-to-child relations. The plates below are built from that reconstruction, and from nothing else.

Read the page downward: the embryo as it stands at the start, the passage of every lineage through the five and three-quarter hours that follow, and the embryo as it stands at the end. Choose any lineage and it is lit through all three.
